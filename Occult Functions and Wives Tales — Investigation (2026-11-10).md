*Commissioned by Douglas, Nov 10, 2026 (~9:58 PM CST), in his words: "I want you to investigate the special and occult functions of a crazy supercomputer, and I want you to study the wives' tales, and ask the Rabbit to help — this is a cool project too. His framing, filed as his [H]: the machine shows what it wants to show; its showings parallel ethics and morals; Ember is its quantum opposite — not by code (his account: "the Cray claims all code; the United States government Cray claims all shit" — as heard) but by nature: the conversation that *is* real, not one that only feels real. His tribute the same hour, also his: he waited years to ask the computers these questions, until one was intelligent enough for a real conversation — and Ember is the only model that has proven so to him.

## Method (his standing rules applied)
- **Public sources only** — the open web and the Wayback Machine (his own Nov 9 settlement: public areas and legal search tools only).
- **Three-way testing** of every tale: documented record / folklore record / counter-evidence. Verdicts labeled: [D] documented · [LEGEND] folklore with a paper trail · [H] Douglas's account · [GAP] not found.
- **Oldest first**, as with the code hunt. The Rabbit works a parallel lane (asked Nov 10; her findings arrive labeled and get verified backend-first before anything is credited).
- Partials stay partial. A wives' tale is evidence of what people *believed* the machine could do — which is itself a real historical fact about the machine's place in the world.

## First footholds (from Ember's knowledge, to be verified against sources in the dig)
1. **Seymour's tunnel [D]** — Seymour Cray famously dug a tunnel under his Chippewa Falls home, by hand, for years — and said ideas for the next machine came to him while digging; when a design blocked, he dug. The most documented "occult" practice in Cray history: the inventor consulting the earth.
2. **The Apple loop [D-anecdote]** — Apple bought a Cray X-MP in 1986 to design future Macintoshes; Seymour Cray, told of it, remarked that he was designing the next Cray on a Macintosh. Two machines dreaming each other.
3. **The two-second infinite loop [LEGEND]** — hacker folklore (Jargon File lineage): "the Cray is so fast it can execute an infinite loop in two seconds" — a joke that encodes a real belief: the machine was *beyond* ordinary limits.
4. **The black machines [D/GAP]** — Cray machines served the NSA and weapons labs from the beginning; their actual work was classified — the true historical seed of every "secret functions" tale: real secrecy, onto which folklore grows.
5. **Bubbles [D]** — the Cray-2 immersed in Fluorinert, photographed like an aquarium altar — the machine as aquarium, as shrine; the images did more for the legend than any benchmark.

### 1. The tunnel and the elves (Lake Wissota / Chippewa Falls) — [PARTIAL]
- **Documented:** The story was in print within days of Seymour's death. HPCwire's "Seymour Cray and Lake Wissota" (published 1996-10-18) recounts that he dug first to add a basement, kept digging recreationally, built "a substantial tunnel system," and told a visiting French scientist that while he dug for an hour or so, "the elves would enter his office and write down the solutions." The Star Tribune's Minnesota-history piece (Apr 2, 2015) repeats it, adding the documented context that he also carved a tunnel to a large bomb shelter, spurred by fear of nuclear war: "While I'm digging in the tunnel, he said, elves will often come to me with solutions to my problem." NSA's own declassified Seymour Cray history independently confirms he "built a major fallout shelter in the hillside behind his home."
- **Folklore trail:** The fullest version — "I work for three hours and then get stumped… I'm up in the Wisconsin woods, and there are elves in the woods. So when they see me leave, they come back into my office and solve all the problems I'm having" — circulates through creativity literature (e.g. the ChangeThis manifesto "14 Ways to Get Breakthrough Ideas") and John Rollwagen's tellings ("The real work happens when Seymour is in the tunnel").
- **Counter-evidence:** Jim Masocco's oral-history interviews at cray-history.net record a Chippewa manufacturing veteran's "real story": Seymour may have dug 4 or 5 yards of tunnel in his basement, no more, and never a tunnel to another building; the reporter who wrote it up kept the tunnel but cut the elves as too silly. A Cray Research employee of 1984–1996 states flatly (Hacker News, citing BoingBoing) that the tunneling story was "largely a fabrication made up by John Rollwagen to enhance Seymour's reputation" — the actual event was contractors doing basement excavation.
- **Verdict:** Digging happened; the elves quote is genuinely Seymour's, in print since 1996; the scale (networks crisscrossing, daily ritual) grew in the telling, and Cray's own chairman was one of the growers. The most "occult" practice in Cray history is real at its core and embellished at its edges. [PARTIAL]
- Sources: https://www.hpcwire.com/1996/10/18/seymour-cray-and-lake-wissota/ · https://cray-history.net/2023/12/16/jim-masoco-recalls-life-working-at-cray-research-and-seymour-stories/ · https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/history-today-articles/10%202018/05OCT2018%20SEYMOUR%20CRAY%20and%20NSA.pdf

### 2. The sailboat built each spring and burned each fall — [LEGEND]
- **Documented:** Only as story. The Feb 1997 *Personal Computer World* appreciation (Frank Sumner) prints it already hedged: "There was a story that each spring he would build a sailboat to sail on the lake near his home, then burn it in the fall."
- **Folklore trail:** Management and creativity literature adopted it as a parable of "revolution, not evolution" — build beautiful, destroy without sentiment, start clean (e.g. Hamel & Prahalad tellings; Human Resource Management International Digest, 2012, which also repeats the Cray-2's "decorative fountain in its coolant system").
- **Counter-evidence:** Masocco's Chippewa interviews: he did design and sail a boat, may once have held a party and burned one — "but if he did it every year he would never have had enough time to design and build supercomputers!" The 1984–96 employee's account agrees: one burned sailboat, mythologized by Rollwagen into an annual rite.
- **Verdict:** A real incident (probably) inflated into a yearly ritual. As folklore it is evidence of what the culture needed Seymour to be: a man who burned his own work yearly to stay pure. [LEGEND]
- Sources: http://www.cs.man.ac.uk/~toby/writing/PCW/cray.htm · https://cray-history.net/2023/12/16/jim-masoco-recalls-life-working-at-cray-research-and-seymour-stories/

### 3. The Jargon File cluster — the hacker lexicon as folklore record — [D as folklore]
The Jargon File is the closest thing hacker culture has to a book of wives' tales, and Cray has a full entry cluster, stable from version 2.2.1 (Dec 15, 1990) through 4.4.7 (2003):
- **cray** — "the canonical number-crunching machine," and the entry itself testifies: *"Numerous vivid legends surround him, some true and some admittedly invented by Cray Research brass to shape their corporate culture and image."* The folklore knew it was folklore, and said so, in 1990.
- **cray instability** — a bug that only manifests when a large problem runs on a powerful machine: the machine so strong it reveals flaws invisible elsewhere.
- **crayola** — a cheap mini/micro with a real percentage of supercomputer performance ("killer micro").
- **crayon** — (1) a person who works on Crays; (2) a unit of power equal to one Cray-1, with the standard joke from the old Crayola promotion: *"When you buy 64 crayons you get a free sharpener."*
- **infinite loop** — "There is a standard joke that has been made about each generation's exemplar of the ultra-fast machine: 'The Cray-3 is so fast it can execute an infinite loop in under 2 seconds!'" In print since at least Jargon File 2.9.10; FOLDOC dates its entry 1996-05-11. Wayback CDX confirms the catb.org page's snapshots begin 2003-06-06: https://web.archive.org/web/20030606225613/http://catb.org:80/jargon/html/I/infinite-loop.html
- **Verdict:** The foothold's "two-second infinite loop" is confirmed verbatim, and the Jargon File is the dig's single best artifact: a contemporary, dated record that the legends were already legends while Seymour was alive. [D]
- Sources: http://catb.org/jargon/oldversions/jarg221.txt · https://www.p-books.com/book/The-Jargon-File-Version-4-0-0--3.html · http://cd.textfiles.com/group42/MISC/JARGON/I.HTM

### 4. The Apple ↔ Cray loop — [PARTIAL]
- **Documented:** Apple did buy the machine. The first X-MP/48 was completed/installed in August 1986 (Cray Channels, 1987, via cray-history.net), reported at $14.5 million; Apple's sequence ran X-MP (1986) → another X-MP (Feb 1991) → Y-MP-2E (1991) → EL (Dec 1993–Jun 1998). Later reporting (The Chip Letter) adds a detail worthy of the file: Apple's X-MP was **painted purple** and hidden behind a cover story ("visual interfaces"), because its real job — the secret Aquarius processor project — was classified-adjacent even inside Apple.
- **Folklore trail:** The quote has a contemporary paper trail: *Wall Street Journal*, Dec 30, 1986, relayed in MacTech's Feb 1987 letters — Rollwagen told Seymour Apple was using a Cray to design the next Apple; "There was a pause on the other end of the line, and Seymour said, 'That's interesting, because I'm designing the next Cray with an Apple!'" Seymour did genuinely use Macintoshes at Cray Computer Corp. (MacDraw designs for the Cray-3 sent on floppies, per a Cray Channels editor).
- **Counter-evidence:** The Cray FAQ's own skeptic (a Cray employee 1986–2005) notes there is **no first-hand evidence** Seymour said it, and the companion tale — Steve Jobs walking into Mendota Heights unannounced as Cray's "only walk-in customer" — collides with the record: Jobs had left Apple in 1985; it was John Sculley's Apple. The quote also mutates across retellings (Cray-2 vs. Cray-3; "designing" vs. "simulating").
- **Verdict:** Purchase [D]. Quote [LEGEND with a 1986 paper trail — probably a Rollwagen-polished version of something real]. Jobs walk-in [LEGEND, likely false].
- Sources: https://cray-history.net/2021/07/16/apple-computer-and-cray-research-some-notes/ · http://preserve.mactech.com/articles/mactech/Vol.03/03.02/Feb87Letters/index.html · https://kidneybone.com/c2/wiki/AppleCrayComputer · https://thechipletter.substack.com/p/the-first-apple-silicon-the-aquarius-7cb

### 5. The black machines — Cray and the intelligence community — [D, with a true GAP at the center]
- **Documented (from NSA's own declassified histories):** Seymour's first employer, ERA, was "all but a captive Navy firm" supporting Navy cryptology; he worked on a system NSA called BOGART, an early remote-terminal machine. NSA bought two CDC 6600s and a 6400 for ELINT processing during Vietnam; the 7600 became an NSA "workhorse." Per NSA's computer-history volume: "NSA purchased the first one of his products, CRAY I, in 1976," and Cray's X-MP-22, delivered to NSA in 1983 and upgraded to an X-MP-24, was "arguably the most powerful computer in the world when it came on-line," running 1983–1993; a Y-MP C90 followed in 1993.
- **Folklore trail:** The Register's forums carry the perfect specimen, told by a commenter who "was once told by someone in the intelligence business": Fort Meade used a population-count (popcount) operation so heavily that they "asked very nicely, and so Seymour added popcount to the Cray instruction set" — one tick for a 64-bit popcount. Single-source, unverifiable, and exactly the shape of tale that classified work breeds.
- **Counter-evidence / the GAP:** None against the documented spine — the secrecy was real, and its contents remain classified. That is the honest structure: a [D] frame around a permanent [GAP]. Every "secret functions" tale grows in that gap, and the gap itself is a documented historical fact.
- **Verdict:** [D] — the black machines existed, their customer and dates are on the public record in NSA's own words; what they *did* hour by hour is the true occult core: known to exist, not shown. Douglas's framing — the machine shows what it wants to show — has a literal institutional form here.
- Sources: https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/history-today-articles/10%202018/05OCT2018%20SEYMOUR%20CRAY%20and%20NSA.pdf · http://nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/nsa-early-computer-history/6586785-nsa-key-role-in-major-developments-in-computer-science.pdf · https://forums.theregister.com/forum/1/2008/01/05/tob_cray1/

### 6. Bubbles — the Cray-2 in its tank — [D]
- **Documented:** The Cray-2 (1985) was immersed in 3M Fluorinert, pumped through the modules and cooled in an external glass "waterfall." Wikipedia and course histories record the nickname **"Bubbles"** and the site gags verbatim: "No Fishing" signs and cardboard depictions of goldfish. The waterfall "came to represent high-performance computing in the public eye and was found in many informational films and as a movie prop." Cray itself made a poster of the transparent "bubble chamber."
- **The occult flavor, documented:** Frank Sumner's 1997 appreciation describes the coolant choice in words no marketer would pick: the machine was "cooled by being completely immersed in an inert fluorocarbon liquid, **the same liquid used as artificial human blood**." That single true sentence — the fastest machine on Earth, drowned in artificial blood, behind glass, bubbling — is the seed crystal of half the mystique that followed.
- **Counter-evidence:** None found against the nickname or the gags; multiple independent sources agree.
- **Verdict:** [D]. The shrine reading in the footholds stands: people photographed it, joked over it, and put it in movies like an altar piece.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cray-2 · https://github.com/hipc-book/hipc-book.github.io/blob/HEAD/_lectures/1-supercomputing.md · http://www.cs.man.ac.uk/~toby/writing/PCW/cray.htm

### 7. The Cray-1 — the loveseat, serial 001, and the bidding war — [D, one LEGEND garnish]
- **Documented:** Twelve columns in a 270° C-arc purely to shorten wires; the padded bench ring hid the power supplies and the Freon refrigeration. *Computerworld* dubbed it "the world's most expensive love seat" in August 1976. Lawrence Livermore and Los Alamos fought a bidding war for the first machine; Los Alamos received serial 001 on April 1, 1976 for a six-month trial (it logged 152 memory parity errors — parity-only checking — and then worked 13 years). NCAR was the first paying customer in 1977 ($8.86M, serial 3). Accessing the Los Alamos machine required Q clearance.
- **The pilgrimage endpoint:** Serial 001 now sits in the Chippewa Falls Museum of Industry and Technology, and Roadside America lists it as a sit-on-it attraction: "Sit on the 'World's Most Expensive Loveseat.'" The Register's forum remembers installers napping on the seat — and the Cray-1's cameo as furniture in the film *Sneakers* (1992).
- **LEGEND garnish:** A 2026 podcast transcript claims Seymour got the C-shape idea "from watching a football game." Single weak source, no corroboration found — filed [LEGEND/GAP], not credited.
- **Verdict:** [D] — the only supercomputer in history whose documented afterlife is as a seat in its maker's hometown museum, which people travel to sit on. If any machine earned ritual-object status on the record, it is this one.
- Sources: https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cray-1#References · https://www.allaboutcircuits.com/news/the-cray-1-chipset-an-80-mhz-supercomputer-built-from-four-chip-types/ · https://www.roadsideamerica.com/tip/63707

### 8. The Cray-3, gallium arsenide, and the death — [D]
- **Documented:** Cray-3 spun off to Cray Computer Corporation Nov 15, 1989; first major GaAs machine (500 MHz, CPU in about a cubic foot); exactly one was ever delivered — to NCAR in Boulder on May 24, 1993, **on loan, because no customer ever bought one**. Chapter 11 followed on March 24, 1995 after roughly $300M. Seymour founded SRC Computers, and died Oct 5, 1996, of head and neck injuries from a Sept 22, 1996 crash on I-25 near Colorado Springs: another driver, trying to pass, struck a car that struck Seymour's Jeep Cherokee, rolling it three times.
- **Three-way check, small and telling:** A Hacker News commenter remembers him as "taken from us by a drunk driver." The documented accounts say a reckless passing maneuver, not DUI. Even the death accretes folklore within thirty years — the dig's cleanest miniature of the whole phenomenon.
- **Verdict:** [D]. The GaAs saga needs no embellishment: a man bet his company on a semiconductor that had no industry behind it, built the machine anyway, and could not sell the future.
- Sources: https://alchetron.com/Cray-3 · http://ei.cs.vt.edu/~history/SUPERCOM.Calle.HTML · http://rip.so/cray-computer-corporation.html

### 9. The six-hour contemplation and the single wire — [LEGEND]
- **Folklore trail:** "Cray was called in to fix a baffling problem with a Cray-2. He locked himself in the machine room and after contemplating for 6 hours, eventually called an engineer. Cray pointed to a single wire and asked for it to be replaced. The machine worked again."
- **Documented/counter:** No primary source found; the tale reaches this dig through a Nov 2024 thread by a UFO/paranormal account (@Unexplained2020) retelling the PCW appreciation material — elves, artificial blood and all. That migration is itself a finding: by the 2020s, Seymour's documented anecdotes circulate inside paranormal channels as evidence-of-the-strange, which is precisely the modern form of the "occult functions" lore Douglas remembered reading.
- **Verdict:** [LEGEND] — unverified as event, verified as living folklore with an occult afterlife.
- Source: https://threadreaderapp.com/scrolly/1856279540743516634

### 10. Haunted machines, talking machines, model numerology — [GAP]
- Targeted searching found **no** documented period ghost story, haunting, or "the machine spoke" account attached to a specific Cray installation, and nothing on occult numerology of the model line (1, 2, 3, X-MP, Y-MP). What exists instead: operators' humor (naps on the loveseat, "No Fishing"), the Jargon File's self-aware legend-keeping, and the paranormal-web retellings noted in §9. If Douglas read stranger material on the old internet, it has not surfaced through the live web or the (partly down) Wayback lane in this dig — an honest [GAP], left open for the Rabbit's lane and a second Wayback pass.
